# Design: Campus Customs Redesign

**Goal:** give shoppers an immersive Yale experience that feels like one continuous page,
even while they move between pages. Shoppers who feel at home and never wait keep
browsing, and shoppers who keep browsing buy.

The look follows [campuscustoms.com](https://www.campuscustoms.com/), adapted rather than
copied.

## What changed, and why it sells

### 1. It looks like Campus Customs and sounds like Yale

- **Changed:**
  - The shop's own navy (`#0c233f`) and steel blue (`#7ba0c5`), Archivo Narrow headings,
    Raleway text, and a pill-shaped search in the header.
  - A top bar: "Officially licensed Yale apparel · 57 Broadway, New Haven".
  - Copy about Old Campus, Sterling, residential colleges, and The Game ("Wear your
    college crest", "Tradition you can wear").
- **Why it sells:**
  - The site looks like the store people already know, so it feels official and safe to
    buy from.
  - Yale gear is bought on pride and nostalgia, and the copy reminds shoppers why they
    came.

### 2. Shop by who it's for

- **Changed:** a second header row:
  - Colleges (11)
  - Sports (17)
  - Graduate Schools (11)
  - Parents & Family (Mom, Dad, Grandma, and more)

  Each menu shows item counts and lists only options that have gear.
- **Why it sells:**
  - People buy Yale gear to show who they are: their college, team, or school, or their
    student's.
  - "Pierson College" or "Yale Dad" is two clicks away, not a scroll through 102 items.
  - No menu option leads to an empty page.

### 3. No waiting, just fades

- **Changed:**
  - The header, footer, and chat never reload; only the page content fades in, in under
    half a second.
  - A product's details load while the shopper points at its card, so clicking opens it
    instantly.
  - There are no spinners or "Loading…" text anywhere.
- **Why it sells:** every wait is a chance to leave. When the site feels like one page,
  shoppers keep clicking through products.

### 4. Products that are easy to judge

- **Changed:**
  - Each card shows the photo, garment type, name, price, and a two-line description.
  - On the product page, the photo stays in view while the shopper chooses color, size,
    and embroidery.
  - "Back to results" returns to their search or chat results.
- **Why it sells:**
  - The price and key facts are visible at a glance.
  - Shoppers can compare items without losing their place.

### 5. A size guide on the Home page

- **Changed:**
  - One chart that switches by sex (men's/unisex or women's), product (T-shirts,
    sweatshirts & hoodies, jackets & fleece), and inches or centimeters.
  - A fit tip for each product type.
  - On a product page, "Size guide" opens the chart already set to that item.
- **Why it sells:**
  - "Will it fit?" is a common reason people hold off on buying clothes online, and the
    guide answers it on the spot.
  - Right-sized orders mean fewer returns.

### 6. Handsome Dan runs the chat

- **Changed:**
  - A "Chat with us" tab is docked at the bottom of the screen.
  - Each time the chat opens, a caricature of Handsome Dan pops up over it: smiling, with
    his tongue out the side, in his navy sweater and bow tie.
  - Every chat opens with "Boola Boola!", which is never repeated during that chat.
- **Why it sells:**
  - A friendly mascot invites questions.
  - The chat answers the questions that hold up a sale: real prices, which sizes are in
    stock, and matching products shown right on the page. It works like a helpful
    student at the shop counter.

## New ways to shop, and why they sell

### 7. See your embroidery before you buy

- **Changed:** as a shopper types embroidery text, it appears on the product photo in the
  spot and thread color they chose (e.g. "Class of 2027" in gold below the Pierson crest).
- **Why it sells:** embroidery is the most custom part of an order and the easiest to
  hesitate on. Seeing it removes the guesswork, and makes the dark-thread warning visible.

### 8. A wishlist family can buy from

- **Changed:** "Share a link" on the Wishlist page. The shopper adds their size, and
  optionally a saved embroidery design, to each item. Family open the link and click "Add
  to my cart".
- **Why it sells:** the student picks and the parent, grandparent, or club pays. Like a
  gift registry, it turns one person's interest into another person's purchase.

### 9. Never a dead end when a size sells out

- **Changed:** a sold-out size can still be picked, and shows similar items in stock in
  that size: same college or team first, then the same kind of garment. Shared wishlists
  and the chat do the same.
- **Why it sells:** 145 of the 612 size slots are sold out. Instead of leaving, the shopper
  gets another item to buy.

### 10. Handsome Dan finds your size

- **Changed:** asked "what size should I get?", the chat asks for the shopper's usual size
  and preferred fit, recommends a size from the size guide, and says whether it's in stock.
- **Why it sells:** "Will it fit?" gets answered in the conversation, which means fewer
  abandoned carts and fewer returns.

### 11. Group orders for teams, clubs, and colleges

- **Changed:** "Group order" on every product page: enter how many of each size (e.g. XS
  × 3, M × 5, XXL × 2), add one embroidery design to every piece, and add them all at once.
- **Why it sells:** custom group orders are what Campus Customs is known for. Ten
  embroidered pieces take one click instead of three trips through the page.

### 12. A Home page that follows the Yale calendar

- **Changed:** the first hero slide and the featured products change with the season:
  move-in, Family Weekend (now), The Game, holiday gifts, winter, Bulldog Days,
  Commencement, and summer.
- **Why it sells:** the right gear at the right time ("Beat Harvard" in November) feels
  current and gives a reason to buy now.

### 13. Complete the look

- **Changed:** each product page ends with matching items: more for the same college,
  team, or family member first (a Pierson crewneck suggests the Pierson tee).
- **Why it sells:** one item becomes an outfit, so baskets get bigger.

### 14. Try it on (phones)

- **Changed:** on a phone, "Try it on" opens the front camera with the garment over the
  shopper's shoulders, including their embroidery. They take a photo (or pick one), drag
  and pinch to adjust it, then save or share it.
- **Why it sells:** seeing yourself in it is the closest thing to the fitting room. A
  shared photo also brings friends to the site. The photo never leaves the phone.

## Checked in the browser

- Page changes fade in with no loading text, and a product the shopper hovered over
  opens instantly.
- "Shop by" menus: Colleges → Pierson shows 2 items; Parents & Family → Dad shows 3.
- Size guide: Women's + T-shirts + centimeters shows bust and waist in cm.
- Chat:
  - Handsome Dan pops up.
  - The greeting starts "Boola Boola!".
  - The reply to "Go Bulldogs! What is this crewneck made of?" doesn't repeat it.
- Phones: the header stacks, with no sideways scrolling.
- Embroidery preview: "Class of 2027" in gold appears below the Pierson crest, and moves
  to the right chest or shoulder when that spot is chosen.
- Sold out: size L of the Pierson crewneck shows the Pierson tee and three crewnecks, all
  in stock in L.
- Group order: XS × 3, M × 5, XXL × 9 (capped at the 2 in stock) adds 10 embroidered
  pieces for $680.00. A second order that would go over stock adds nothing.
- Shared link: "Sam's Yale wishlist" shows Sam's note and sizes. The in-stock hoodie is
  added in one click, and the sold-out crewneck shows similar items in Sam's size.
- Chat sizing: "I usually wear a women's medium and like it a little roomy" → a unisex
  M, in stock. "Do you have this in a large?" → sold out, with three similar items.
- Seasons: Family Weekend today; adding `?date=2026-11-20` shows "Beat Harvard" and
  football gear.
- Try it on (phone size): the hoodie sits under the face with the hood behind the head,
  "Smith 27" embroidery included. Dragging moves it. The button is hidden on a laptop.
